# Everfresh grow chamber: the short version

## What it does

Everfresh is a sealed glass cabinet that keeps one tropical tree (a *Cojoba*, "monkey earring tree") warm, humid and lit around the clock. A small Wi-Fi controller runs it and texts Ara when something breaks. The tree is 16 in tall today, hopefully 28 in within a year, in a 2 gallon pot (room for 4).

**What's inside**

- **Heater:** ceramic heat emitters (the reptile kind) in a guarded housing the fan blows through. It dries any leaf it can see directly.
- **Mister:** an ultrasonic fogger in a small water basin, fed from a 2 gallon tank you refill from outside.
- **Circulation fan:** always on, low speed. It mixes the air and carries mist and heat around the box.
- **Exhaust fan:** high up. Room air comes in through a screened vent down low. Only runs in bursts.
- **Two sensors:** one at leaf height inside, one at the intake outside.
- **Grow light:** not picked yet. It could be a panel on top or tall bars standing upright.
- **Electronics:** controller, relays and power supplies. Ara builds and wires these himself.

**Worth knowing**

- **It's wet in there.** 70 to 95% humidity all the time, with mist several times an hour. Every inside surface will see condensation, so nothing inside can rot, swell or mold.
- **Sealing matters most.** On the current chamber, closing one gap stabilized the humidity more than any software change. The door is the biggest leak.
- **It needs real heat.** Single glass holding 76°F in a 62°F room needs about 150 W for a 24 × 18 × 52 in box. Today's 25 W heater can't keep up.
- **Water goes down, electronics stay up.** Electronics live in a dry bay, sealed from the humid zone, never below water.
- **Lessons from the current chamber:** a leaf touching the sensor fooled the controller; an oversized exhaust hole let air loop back around the fan; a cheap fan seized in the humidity; and the reservoir once ran dry.

## What we have in mind

**The cabinet is yours.** Layout, frame, joinery, materials and the look. A polished take on today's one-tree stack is great. So is something wider for two plants side by side, or a small nook for cuttings. Ara leans toward single-pane glass or acrylic, glass on as many sides as works, and something that reads as furniture.

**What it has to get right**

- **Zones:** electronics bay on top or to the side, plants in the middle, water at the bottom. Exhaust air goes straight out to the room, not through the electronics.
- **Floor:** a sealed pan that slopes to a drain or a pull-out tray. It catches spills and the condensation running off the glass.
- **Door:** one big door with a continuous gasket and a latch that pulls it tight.
- **Exhaust:** a backdraft flap, so a warm box doesn't vent itself through the fan hole all winter.
- **Cables:** none visible. Run them in chases in the frame, with one panel of keyed plugs where every part connects, so anything lifts out with one plug.
- **Easy care:** refill water from outside, empty the floor pan, and swap a fan, heater or sensor in minutes, without moving the plant.
- **Moves when it must:** it'll stand for years, but should come apart with hand tools into pieces that fit in a car.
- **Quiet:** it shares a lived-in room, so fans are mounted so nothing hums.
